PORTFOLIO PLANNING

Understanding the User Experience

Defining what visitors should be able to accomplish when exploring my future professional portfolio.

01 — USER NEEDS

Designing Around the Visitor

My primary audience consists of recruiters and hiring managers who want to learn about my professional background, technical skills, education, and projects.

To make the portfolio useful and easy to navigate, I developed five user stories that describe the main tasks visitors should be able to complete. Each story includes acceptance criteria that will help determine whether the portfolio successfully meets those needs.

01
INTRODUCTION

Learn About the Candidate

As a recruiter, I want to view an introduction and professional background, so that I can quickly understand the candidate's interests, education, and career goals.

Acceptance Criteria

  • The homepage includes a brief professional introduction.
  • The candidate's interests in software development, AI, and UX are clearly communicated.
  • Education and relevant background are easy to find.
  • Navigation makes it easy to access other sections.
02
PROJECTS

Explore Projects

As a hiring manager, I want to browse the candidate's projects, so that I can evaluate their technical abilities, creativity, and problem-solving skills.

Acceptance Criteria

  • Projects are displayed using organized cards or sections.
  • Each project includes a title, description, and relevant images or screenshots.
  • Projects identify the technologies and tools used.
  • Visitors can open a project to view additional details.
03
TECHNICAL SKILLS

Understand Technical Skills

As a recruiter, I want to review the candidate's technical skills, so that I can determine whether their abilities match the requirements of a position.

Acceptance Criteria

  • Skills are organized into understandable categories.
  • Categories include programming, AI and automation, and UX/UI design.
  • Relevant tools and technologies are listed.
  • The skills section is easy to locate and read.
04
UX AND DESIGN

Review UX and Design Experience

As a hiring manager interested in UX or product development, I want to see examples of the candidate's design and research work, so that I can evaluate their understanding of user-centered design.

Acceptance Criteria

  • The portfolio includes relevant UX/UI projects.
  • Projects explain the problem, design process, and outcome.
  • Visual examples, such as Figma prototypes, wireframes, or research findings, are included when available.
  • The presentation demonstrates an understanding of usability and human-centered design.
05
CONTACT

Contact the Candidate

As a recruiter, I want to find the candidate's contact information and professional links, so that I can reach out about potential opportunities.

Acceptance Criteria

  • Contact information is accessible through the navigation or footer.
  • The portfolio includes a professional email contact.
  • Links to relevant professional profiles, such as LinkedIn and GitHub, are available.
  • All links work correctly.

02 — DESIGN PRIORITIES

What These User Stories Tell Me

The user stories highlight the importance of making a professional portfolio informative, accessible, and easy to navigate. Visitors should be able to quickly understand who I am, explore my work, evaluate my skills, and contact me.

These requirements will guide the development of the portfolio and help ensure that design decisions support the needs of its intended audience.
